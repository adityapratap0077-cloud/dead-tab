Standards

Editorial policy

A museum of forgotten things has one asset: being believed. These are the rules that protect that.

Source standards

Exhibits are built from credible sources, in this order of preference: primary material (the thing itself, archived pages, official announcements), institutional and academic sources, reputable journalism, and only then community accounts, which are labelled as such. Every exhibit carries a sources section with titles, publishers, links, and the date we accessed them. We never invent a citation, a quotation, or a statistic.

Evidence statuses

Every exhibit and major claim carries one of these labels:

  • Verified — confirmed by a primary source or multiple independent credible sources.
  • Documented — supported by credible published sources; no primary source survives or is accessible.
  • Partially documented — some facts are sourced; other parts rest on thinner or single-source reporting.
  • Uncertain — reported or remembered, but we could not confirm it. Published only when the uncertainty itself is part of the story, and always flagged.
  • Recollection — a personal memory, published as oral history in the teller's voice. True to their experience; not presented as documented fact.

Corrections

Errors, once known, are corrected in the exhibit itself, and the change is recorded with its date in the museum's correction log. To submit a correction, contact a founder on LinkedIn (see the About page) with the claim, the problem, and a source. Corrections are credited to their contributor where they wish.

Images and copyright

Old does not mean free. We reproduce an image only when its licence or permission status is verified and recorded in our image-rights register, with creator, source, licence, attribution, and the date checked. Where rights are unclear, we use an original illustration or a clearly labelled placeholder and link to the artifact at its home instead. Rights holders who want material corrected or removed can reach us through the About page; valid requests are honoured promptly. This policy page itself is our commitment, not legal advice.

Oral histories

Interviews and submitted memories are published with the teller's permission, credited as they choose, and edited only for length and clarity, never to change meaning. Where a memory conflicts with the documented record, both are shown and the difference is named.

Uncertain claims and open cases

In open cases, established facts, other people's claims, available evidence, disputed points, alternative explanations, and unknowns are kept in separate, labelled sections. Repetition never upgrades a claim into a fact here.

Sponsored content

DEAD TAB carries no sponsored content at launch. If sponsorship ever exists, it will be labelled on the item itself, and sponsors will never control editorial conclusions.

Privacy and safety

We do not publish private messages, personal data, or material that identifies private individuals in ways that could harm them. We never encourage contacting, identifying, or investigating private people. Usernames of public historical interest are handled carefully and in context.

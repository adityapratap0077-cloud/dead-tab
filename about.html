About

A museum for the internet that raised us

DEAD TAB preserves the forgotten stories of the internet: the websites that shut down, the communities that scattered, the usernames, forwards, and rituals that shaped how a generation learned to be online. We begin with India, because that is the internet we know from inside, and we connect it to the global story.

Why it exists

The modern internet is a feed: infinite, personal, and amnesiac. What came before it was stranger and, in some ways, richer: shared computers, handles instead of real names, communities that lived on a single server and died with it. When a platform closes, that world does not move house. It evaporates. DEAD TAB exists so that at least the stories survive, researched properly and told well.

What we preserve

Lost websites and what they meant. Digital identities and the selves people invented. Memes and forwards, traced to their sources where we can. Digital artifacts: the sounds, screens, and formats of an era. And open cases: the mysteries where the honest exhibit is the map of what is not known.

How an exhibit is made

Every exhibit follows the same path: research from credible sources, a recorded source register, a draft, a fact-check pass, an image-rights check, and only then publication. Claims we cannot support are labelled uncertain or left out. Personal memories are welcome and are labelled as recollections, never dressed up as documented fact. The full standards live on the editorial policy page.

Who runs it

DEAD TAB is built and maintained by two founders working part-time. Aditya Pratap looks after the technical side: the site itself, the archive's structure, and keeping it fast and free to run. Nandini Raj leads research and storytelling: interviews, source collection, and the English and Hindi writing that turns records into stories. Both fact-check everything; both decide what belongs in the museum.
